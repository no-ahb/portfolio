Prepare for a physical room

The archive also contains speaker-test patches, REAPER routing templates, and a Max/OSC switch bridge. Audium supplied the theatre and routing infrastructure; the speaker tester credits Matt Wright. My work was the composition, material preparation, and live performance within that system.

I composed and performed Organ Music for Audium’s inaugural New Voices residency in San Francisco. Across 24 live performances in total darkness, sound moved between the body and the theatre. Transducers coupled sound to my throat; the room’s speaker system extended the composition around the audience.

The work joined sound-making, signal routing, and live spatial performance. A sound’s location and the material carrying it were part of the composition.

The room as an instrument

In darkness, listeners encounter distance, movement, and physical vibration without a visible object explaining them. The theatre becomes the interface: a sound can be local and bodily, or spread through the surrounding space.

My contribution was the composition and its performance within Audium’s existing infrastructure. The theatre and its speaker system are Audium’s work. The New Voices programme brought original compositions and custom performance controls into that environment.

Resynthesizing recorded material

Alongside the spatial work, I experimented with concatenative synthesis using AudioGuide. A target recording supplies a structure; the software searches a corpus for small segments with selected acoustic similarities, then assembles those segments into a new sound.

The project configurations make that search a compositional decision. One narrows candidates by perceptual spectral centroid, then matches MFCCs. Another restricts the available material to bright or quiet fragments. Segment envelopes change how clearly the joins can be heard.

These are configurations of an existing analysis and synthesis tool. The choices of source material, matching criteria, and envelopes shape the result. This was an offline sound-making process; it should be understood separately from live spatial control.

Routing and rehearsal

The project archive includes REAPER routing templates, ambisonic decoders, speaker-test patches, and a Max/OSC bridge for addressing Audium’s switching system. Together they document the preparation needed to work with a room whose outputs have different positions and acoustic responses.

Speaker testing uses a patch by Matt Wright. This sits alongside the venue’s routing infrastructure and the project-specific configurations used to prepare the composition.
