What makes Skowhegan work

Color tells you where you are. Type and thin rules do most of the organizing. Photographs share the page's color until you ask to see them.

Evidence: your 2:04 screen recording, plus the live site's CSS and browser behavior inspected on 01 October 2026. Hex values below are source tokens, not samples from compressed video. The recording includes the homepage and several interior pages.

The main text is black (#000000). Each area gets a light, fairly saturated ground. Brown and lavender form a separate, darker pair for the footer and menu. These colors are tied to categories, so revisiting a category returns you to a familiar field.

Source: Skowhegan theme stylesheet and homepage feature attributes.

02 / The transitions are pointer-led

At roughly 00:00–00:25, the homepage moves through green, cream, white, yellow, and pale lavender. Each feature has a category color. Hovering a feature changes the whole homepage field; leaving it returns the field to white. I verified that return by moving the pointer to the margin without scrolling. Scrolling carries new features under a stationary pointer, which makes the change look tied to scroll position.

The homepage background uses a 300 ms color transition. It fades between flat fills. The interior pages hold their category colors while you scroll; the dark footer supplies a clear ending. This gives the site continuity without continuous drifting gradients.

For yours: keep one color for instruments and installations, another for software, and a quiet index color. The studio also tests a scroll variation and fixed section colors. Those two modes are proposals, not claims about the reference.

03 / Images are part of the ground

The source uses grayscale() with mix-blend-mode: multiply. White areas pick up the page color while dark marks remain dark. A second, unfiltered image fades over the first in 300 ms on hover. The effect is more integrated than a black-and-white photograph on a colored rectangle.

For yours: try this on North Sea, Suspension, and recording-session photographs. Keep diagrams, spectrograms, and product screenshots in original color when their colors carry information. The mockup adds keyboard focus and a click-to-keep-color option for touch and closer inspection.

04 / The text has more than one voice

Reference

Large, regular sans-serif

Neue Haas Unica Regular, with Helvetica as fallback. Source sizes include 68 px display text, 30–34 px secondary headings, 18–19 px reading/navigation text, and 14 px small text. Exact rendered sizes vary by breakpoint and element.

Reference

Small, denser serif

Times New Roman handles editorial passages, secondary navigation, and captions. This change in family creates much of the apparent change in weight. The CSS loads regular and italic Unica; rich-text emphasis requests weight 500.

At 00:40–00:50, a large centered question sits above a much narrower paragraph, then full-width expandable rows. At 01:15–01:30, the Library alternates large editorial titles with compact archive rows. Scale, line length, spacing, and alignment create hierarchy even when the type remains regular.

For yours: keep Instrument Sans for the introduction, project names, and controls. Try Times for the descriptions and captions. Use 400 for large names, 500 for row labels or small emphasis, and reserve 700 for a deliberate accent. Keep technical labels in the sans-serif. The Typography controls let you compare that pairing with your existing all-sans treatment.

05 / Useful structures to borrow

  1. Feature first, index later. Give North Sea and the main installations room to be seen. Follow them with a compact project index so returning visitors can find a specific project.
  2. Expand for context, open for depth. The School's question rows and Library archive reveal detail in place. Use an expandable project row for a short explanation and a separate, explicit project link. The mockup includes both.
  3. Stable columns and thin rules. The archive uses repeated columns for title and metadata. On your site, format and material/system are more useful starting columns than dates inferred from update timestamps. The mockup uses verified descriptions from your project files.
  4. Filter only where it helps. Installation, Performance, and Software filters offer a quick way through the index. Keep this secondary to the selected work.
  5. Keep position and identity. Skowhegan's persistent wordmark and section navigation help orient long pages. Your name and a small project index can provide that continuity without copying the vertical letter treatment.
  6. Give controls a stable surface. Preserve readable labels and faithful colors in your working audio interfaces. Let the surrounding page carry the atmosphere; avoid hiding essential controls behind hover.

06 / Directions for your site

Skowhegan: the exact reference colors are the most direct comparison. Mineral: cooler blue-grey for the physical work, dull yellow for listening, lilac for software; a closer connection to steel and seawater. Warm circuit: paper, copper, butter, and lilac, with brown text. Paper & ink is a restrained control for judging how much color the images actually need.

My starting recommendation is Mineral + blended photographs + hover-driven color, keeping the introduction pale. Compare the scroll variation if you want the color sequence to feel more authored. There is no need to turn every section into a different hue; the role controls let multiple areas share a color.

Other source pages: The School, Lecture Archive, Support. The mockup uses your existing project copy and images. This study is a separate local route; it does not apply a new palette to the main portfolio.
