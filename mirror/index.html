Mirror

Six microphone perspectives translated into one vibrating surface

2024

  • electroacoustics
  • physical computing
  • fabrication

Six inputs share one material.

Microphone placement determines what reaches the instrument. The plate's response is shared: the six electrical paths meet through the vibration of a single sheet of steel.

Signal-path schematic. Positions are explanatory, not a measured plate-mode diagram.

Collect different perspectives

Six microphone positions make the surrounding room a distributed input. Each position hears a different balance of nearby sound and reverberation before any signal reaches the plate.

Excite without contact

Voice coils drive the steel electromagnetically. The physical surface supplies the resonance, and all six paths interact through it. The installation photographs show the plate, suspension, and exposed connections.

Make the working surface the image

The etched musical staves sit on the same steel that vibrates. The visible object and the acoustic component share a surface. The close-up below records that construction.

I built Mirror as a six-channel plate reverb instrument for As Long as a Piece of String at Sarabande. Six microphones around the gallery fed voice coils that excited a steel plate without touching it. Sound in the room became movement in a shared physical surface.

Designing the signal path

The starting point was the EMT-140 plate reverb. Mirror extends that principle into an installation: several microphone positions collect different perspectives on the same space, and the steel combines those inputs through its own resonances.

The signal path is simple to follow: room sound, microphones, electromagnetic excitation, plate vibration. The physical plate supplies the resonant behavior.

The material as a processor

A plate responds differently across its surface and across frequency. The result depends on the steel and how it is excited. Microphone placement determines what enters that system; the vibrating surface reshapes what comes out.

Four columns of empty musical staves are etched into the steel. The surface is both a working component and the visible face of the instrument.

A room that supplies its own input

Visitors encounter a system already listening to its surroundings. Their position and the sound around them belong to the same acoustic situation as the microphones and plate.

Mirror was presented at Sarabande in London in 2024. The documentation below shows the built instrument in its installation setting.
