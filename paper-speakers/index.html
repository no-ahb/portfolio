Distribute a changing pitch field.

The SuperCollider archive exposes how the installation's sound material was generated. Harmonic and subharmonic distributions produce sets of frequencies; values outside the configured band are folded by octaves. Then the routine staggers their entrances and assigns outputs.

Let pitch affect pacing

The routine computes the average frequency of a cluster, normalizes it within the working band, and uses a sine-shaped curve to set pacing. Pitch selection therefore also changes the composition's timescale.

Avoid a fixed entrance order

The source shuffles pitches and output assignments. Notes in the patch ask for more staggered entrances and more variation in held tones: evidence of listening back and revising the density of the result.

Keep software and installation counts distinct

The installation has 34 handmade speakers. The archived final-draft patch initializes 16 voices and 26 output slots, then varies density. The diagram reproduces a 16-value pitch calculation; it is not a recovered wiring plan for all 34 speakers.

Paper Speakers is a 34-channel installation built with handmade speakers of paper and copper wire. I developed it in 2023–24 and presented it in As Long as a Piece of String at Sarabande, London.

The speaker itself is the object under investigation. Its membrane, construction, and placement are part of the sound system the audience encounters.

Making the transducers visible

The paper and copper make the construction legible. A visitor can look closely at the same surface that carries the vibration. The installation connects the electrical signal to a fragile, visible material.

Working at the scale of a room

The project combines the construction of individual transducers with the arrangement of many sound sources in space. Those are two connected design scales: how a single object produces sound, and how a listener encounters the collection.
